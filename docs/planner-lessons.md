# Troubleshooting planner: lessons learned

[Back to portfolio](../README.md)

## What I wanted

A note-preparation tool handled tasks with an existing diagnosis. I hoped a planner could suggest next troubleshooting steps for other tasks and save more preparation time.

## What did not fit

People can take different reasonable approaches to troubleshooting. A suggestion that seems useful to its author can feel prescriptive to the person doing the work.

In repeated trials, I found that the task assignee was not always the person who would actually perform the repair. Tailoring suggestions to the assignee therefore did not reliably solve the problem.

## What I decided

I paused the planned integration. I wanted to save technicians time, not tell them how to troubleshoot. A script can work as designed and still be a poor fit for how people work.

## What I would check first next time

- Who actually performs the task, rather than just who is assigned to it.
- Which steps people agree can be standardized.
- What evidence a suggestion needs.
- Where the tool should stop and let the technician choose.

The proposed integration was not rolled out as part of the coordinated toolkit. This page does not rank technicians or treat disagreement alone as proof that their diagnosis was wrong. Exact trial counts and internal examples are kept out of this review copy.
